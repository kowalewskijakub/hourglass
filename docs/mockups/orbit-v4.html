Hourglass · Orbit — product mockup v2

In orbit, above your day.

The full app in the Orbit direction, now in both skies: the app follows the real sun — before local sunset you fly the day side over the blue marble; after dark, the night side over city lights. Amber is the only working color; the cycle reads as dots, never digits; and the workday — clock in, break, clock out — is a first-class loop with its own states.

07 · Motion

How it moves

The pulse

The now-point breathes once per second while anything is recording — focus or plain clocked-in work. Paused or on break, it holds still.

Clock-in is a launch

On clock-in the arc ignites from the limb and the camera eases up to orbit altitude — a 1.2 s one-time ceremony.

Session end is a dawn

Completion flashes the dawn glow across the rim; a cycle dot lights; the finished segment cools from Dawn to Ember and locks into the log.

The sun is real

Black/Blue Marble textures (public domain) on a SceneKit sphere, terminator from real solar position, camera above your coarse location. The sky crossfades through golden hour at actual sunset.

Scrub the day

Drag along the orbit to review the day; each segment lifts slightly under the finger and shows its times. Release springs back to now.

Reduced motion

With Reduce Motion, the pulse, launch, and parallax collapse to opacity cross-fades; the scene renders as a static still.

Orbit keeps every locked decision — SF semibold numerals, no emoji, one working accent, cycle progress as dots, Pomodoro and Workday unified on one orbit — and maps onto the existing architecture: the scene is one SwiftUI view over PomodoroEngine + WorkdayTracker, the popover reuses it with a landscape camera, and Stats/Settings restyle the shared views that already exist.
